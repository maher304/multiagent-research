                   Dataset Selection and Suitability Analysis
    Governed Multi-Agent AI for Autonomous E-Commerce and Accounting Operations



                                            10 October 2026


                                                 Abstract
          This report evaluates the local Olist Brazilian E-Commerce dataset and Online Retail
      II for a governed multi-agent e-commerce and accounting prototype. All nine Olist CSV
      tables and the two worksheets in the local Online Retail II workbook were inspected without
      modifying source files. Olist comprises 9 tables, including 99,441 orders, 112,650 order-item
      rows, 103,886 payment rows, and 32,951 products. Online Retail II contains 1,067,371 line
      records in two annual worksheets and the expected eight transaction fields. Olist supports
      broader linked order and marketplace workflows; Online Retail II provides a much larger
      product-line transaction history. Neither dataset provides complete inventory movements, a
      general ledger, explicit governance policies, or verified fraud labels. The datasets are therefore
      suitable for transaction replay, rule-based prototype checks, and auditable derived summaries,
      subject to careful handling of missingness, duplicate rows, and return/cancellation semantics.
      They must be integrated through independent source adapters; actual records must not be
      joined across datasets.


1     Introduction and research context
The project studies a prototype with orchestration, e-commerce, inventory, accounting, risk,
governance, and audit agents. Public transaction datasets can supply evidence for replayable
workflows, but they do not by themselves instantiate agents or business controls. This assessment
separates observable source fields from derived analyses, external requirements, and prototype
behavior.


2     Objectives and method
The objective is to select and characterize locally available data for the research prototype. The
inspection script recursively scanned datasets/ for CSV, Excel, and JSON files, read CSVs
as UTF-8 with BOM support and detected delimiters, and processed the Excel workbook as
OOXML. It calculated dimensions, field names, missingness, distinct non-empty values, numeric
ranges, exact duplicate rows, and selected cross-table key containment checks. All reported
counts are based on local files and are reproducible from the included script and machine-readable
artifacts. Numeric-looking codes are not automatically treated as measures. The comparison uses
qualitative ratings: High means direct, broad field support for the stated prototype task; Medium
means useful but partial fields or substantial derived logic; Low means a material core capability
is absent; Unknown means evidence or terms were not verified. For licensing, High indicates
a verified source license that permits reuse with stated conditions; Medium indicates verified
terms with meaningful use conditions; Unknown means the license was not verified. These are
task-relative judgments, not universal dataset rankings.

4    Olist dataset: structure and empirical findings
The observed schema consists of customer location records; geolocation; order status/timestamps;
item-level product, seller, price, and freight; payment method/installments/value; review
score/comments/timestamps; product dimensions/category; seller location; and a category
translation table. The files provide a relational view, rather than one flat fact table. The
order_id and customer_id columns each have 99,441 distinct non-empty values in their re-
spective 99,441-row tables; product_id has 32,951 distinct values in 32,951 product rows and
seller_id has 3,095 distinct values in 3,095 seller rows. These candidate keys are unique
by observed cardinality. The customer table has 96,096 distinct customer_unique_id values,
showing that this field identifies repeat customer groupings rather than a one-row-per-order key.
Olist has 99,441 order identifiers and 99,441 customer records. The candidate customer and
order keys have no duplicate key values, as confirmed by the key-check artifact. The order-item
table has 112,650 rows, while its 98,666 distinct order IDs are all present in orders. The payment
table has 99,440 distinct order IDs, all present in orders. The review table has 98,690 distinct
order IDs; 17 distinct keys do not occur among the order IDs. These checks are calculated
on observed non-empty identifiers and do not mean that every order has every kind of child
record. Candidate key checks further show that the composite (order_id, order_item_id) is
unique across 112,650 item rows and (order_id, payment_sequential) is unique across 103,886
payment rows; the single order_id field is not unique in those child tables. These duplicate-excess
and missing-key checks are provided in primary_key_checks.csv. Reviews have no separate
review identifier in the observed schema, so no review primary key is asserted. Order items
expose 32,951 distinct product IDs and 3,095 distinct seller IDs, all contained in the products
and sellers keys respectively.
    Olist item prices range from 0.85 to 6,735.00 and freight values range from 0.00 to 409.68
in the source currency (Brazilian real is implied by the Brazilian marketplace context; the
CSV itself has no explicit currency-code field). Payment values range from 0.00 to 13,664.08.

5    Online Retail II: structure and empirical findings
The workbook has eight fields: Invoice, StockCode, Description, Quantity, InvoiceDate,
Price, Customer ID, and Country. It contains 1,067,371 data rows across two sheets, with
12,133 exact duplicate line rows detected in the raw cell values (6,865 and 5,268 by worksheet).
Negative quantities occur in 22,950 rows (12,326 and 10,624 by sheet), with no zero quantities.
There are 19,494 rows whose invoice identifier has the documented cancellation prefix (10,206
and 9,288 by sheet), representing 4,592 and 3,836 distinct cancellation invoice identifiers within
each worksheet. Zero prices occur in 6,202 rows (3,687 and 2,515), and five rows have negative
prices (three and two). These counts identify records for contextual review; they are not fraud
or error determinations. The first sheet has 2,928 missing descriptions (0.557%) and 107,927
missing customer IDs (20.540%). The second has 1,454 missing descriptions (0.268%) and 135,080
missing customer IDs (24.927%). Invoice, stock code, quantity, invoice date, unit price, and
country are populated throughout the inspected rows. A customer identifier therefore cannot be
assumed for every transaction.
    The source’s date values span 1 December 2009 to 9 December 2011, consistent with UCI’s
dataset description. Each worksheet has 8 columns. The first contains 28,816 distinct invoice cell
values, 4,631 stock-code values, 40 countries, and 4,383 non-empty customer IDs. The second
contains 25,900 invoice values, 4,070 stock codes, 38 countries, and 4,372 non-empty customer
IDs. Because invoice identifiers are present in both sheets and the same invoice value might
recur across partitions, totals across sheets require global recomputation rather than adding
the sheet-level distinct counts. Quantity ranges are -9,600 to 19,152 (first sheet) and -80,995 to
80,995 (second). Price ranges include negative and unusually large values: -53,594.36 to 25,111.09
and -11,062.06 to 38,970.00. These values merit review and are not interpreted as errors without
source business context. Invoice IDs beginning with C are a documented cancellation marker;
that is a transaction status convention, not evidence of fraud. Negative quantities may represent
returns or corrections; zero/negative or very large prices may reflect adjustments, special lines,
or data-quality issues. The inspection artifacts report raw-value ranges and do not silently filter
them.

6    Data quality and schema interpretation
For CSVs, blank fields were counted as missing. For Excel, blank cells were counted within each
worksheet. Exact duplicate detection compares complete decoded field values (or workbook cell
values) and counts repeated rows after their first occurrence. The high geolocation duplicate
count is salient and should be assessed in a future pipeline using key semantics and source
documentation, not automatically removed. Identifiers must be handled as strings even where
they contain digits; quantity and price are numeric measures, while invoice dates require proper
spreadsheet date decoding. The included quality file provides missing count and percent, distinct
count, and numeric extrema by field. Date values stored as Excel serials are not misreported as
calendar timestamps in that raw extrema table.

9    Independent adapters and canonical schema
The datasets must remain logically independent. The primary design is one adapter per source,
producing a shared event interface without joining records. A minimal canonical event may
contain source_dataset, source_record_key, event_kind, event_date, item_key, quantity,
unit_amount, and currency_code. source_dataset and source keys are mandatory lineage.
Olist can map order_id to an order event, order_item_id to a line key, product_id to an item
key, price and freight_value to separate amounts, and payment values to payment events.
The currency code is not present and must be configured or left unavailable. Retail II maps
invoice, stock code, quantity, invoice date, and unit price; invoice cancellation status can be
derived from the documented C prefix. Customer IDs are optional. A canonical customer key
remains source-scoped. Missing values are represented as null with an explicit Not available
in source dataset explanation, never fabricated.
    A second, optional layer can normalize event/line concepts and aggregation units for com-
parative experiments. It is schema normalization only. The numeric order/invoice IDs, product
codes, customer identifiers, countries, and date semantics are source-specific and must never be
treated as common business identities. Any mapping beyond the direct field correspondences
above requires an explicit versioned assumption.


10    Accounting and inventory boundaries
Olist contains item prices, freight amounts, payment values, payment methods, and installment
counts. This supports candidate transaction-level summaries and consistency checks, but rows
are not a complete double-entry general ledger: there is no verified account mapping, tax/fee
structure, settlement statement, or general-ledger posting history. Retail II offers quantity and
unit price for invoice lines but does not provide payment/settlement data or explicit currency
code; UCI documents its unit prices in sterling. Neither dataset tracks stock-on-hand over time,
receipts, transfers, reservations, or a complete return-to-stock process. Inventory balances must
therefore be simulated or supplied from an external inventory source.

11     Risk, governance, audit, and coordination limits
Unusual amounts, negative quantities, cancellations, missing identifiers, and referential anomalies
can trigger review rules. A flagged row is not a confirmed fraud case. Neither inspected schema
provides a verified fraud outcome label. Governance policies (thresholds, approval authority,
segregation of duties, privacy retention) are not source data and must be explicitly defined and
versioned by the prototype. Auditability can be implemented through repeatable source-key
checks, immutable input manifests, run identifiers, and agent decision logs; these capabilities
are not automatically present in the files. Orchestration behavior is entirely prototype logic.
Historical data can drive deterministic replays, but it cannot evidence autonomous coordination
unless the prototype emits and evaluates its own traces.

12     Provenance, licensing, and usage
The Olist listing on Kaggle identifies the dataset title/provider, describes approximately 100,000
orders, and lists nine CSV files [2]. Its current license terms were not independently confirmed from
the accessible primary data card during this inspection; verify those terms before redistributing
data or derived artifacts that reproduce source content. Secondary source claims about a specific
Olist license are not treated as a verified permission here. UCI identifies Online Retail II as
a dataset by Daqing Chen, describes its two-year transaction scope, provides the workbook,
DOI, and states CC BY 4.0 [1]. Attribution is required under that license. This report does
not provide legal advice; users should retain dataset attribution and check current terms before
redistribution.


13     Recommended prototype configuration
Use Olist as the primary end-to-end workflow fixture because its local relational tables jointly cover
order status, customers, order lines, payments, products, sellers, and reviews. Use Online Retail
II as an independent transaction-line replay fixture for quantity/price patterns, cancellations,
and return-like observations. Operate each through a source-specific adapter and shared, source-
scoped event interface. Implement inventory balances, accounting ledgers, governance policy
checks, risk thresholds, audit trails, and orchestration as explicit prototype modules; label
simulated outputs. Preserve raw inputs and document each derived metric and exclusion.


14     Threats to validity and limitations
The inspected files may not represent the provider’s latest release. Field typing is a heuristic, and
source semantics can exceed what column names establish. Exact duplicate counts depend on
decoded cell representation; duplicates are not automatically erroneous. The distinct-key joins
test membership, not row-level referential integrity in every direction, cardinality constraints,
temporal validity, or business completeness. Excel serial timestamps require workbook date-
format interpretation. License verification is stronger for the UCI file than for Olist. Qualitative
ratings depend on prototype objectives and should be revisited if the required task changes. No
cross-source business identity is established.


15     Conclusion
Both local datasets are usable for governed multi-agent workflow prototyping when their evidence
boundaries are respected. Olist provides the broader relational workflow context, while Online
Retail II contributes a larger two-year line-level invoice history. A dual-adapter configuration
preserves each source’s semantics and supports controlled comparative replays without asserting
false equivalence. Neither source supplies the stock history, complete accounting records, explicit
governance rules, confirmed fraud labels, or agent coordination needed to claim production
autonomy; these must be externally provided or explicitly simulated and audited.
